## The 60-Second Verdict (Opening thoughts?)
1-2 strong paragraphs, 150–200 words.

Keep it tight, 1–2 strong paragraphs max.

Who is this for? [1–2 sentences]
Who should skip it? [1–2 sentences]
What it feels like solo. [1–2 sentences describing tension, pacing, and overall vibe]
Bottom line. [Clear, confident stance.]

This answers: What does this game feel like?

It’s about: Immediate tone
- First emotional read
- Core identity
- The vibe
- It should feel slightly instinctive and atmospheric.

Example focus:
- “This is a tight, punishing efficiency puzzle.”
- “This feels colder and more mechanical than I expected.”
- “This surprised me with how tense it is.”

It’s about character.

End it with a sentence that naturally opens the door to deeper discussion.

Example transition line:

> Here’s what that actually looks like at the table.

That line flows beautifully into the next section.

One picture. Full table layout or mid-game shot.

## Game at a Glance (Overview?)
Clean bullet list

This section should feel almost technical.

Example:
Players: 1–4 (dedicated solo mode)
True Solo Time: 75–90 minutes
Setup / Teardown: 10 / 8 minutes
Weight: Medium-Heavy
Core Mechanisms: Worker placement, resource conversion, contract fulfillment
Publisher / Designer: X / Y

No prose here.

## How It Plays
Full paragraph(s). 350–400 words.

This is your core identity section.

Use 2–4 tight paragraphs.
No bullets.
This is where nuance lives:
- pacing
- tension
- flow
- frustration
- decision space

Focus only on:
- What the solo mode actually does
- Automa behavior (if applicable)
- Decision space
- Tension & pacing
- Mental overhead
- Flow disruptions (if any)

Avoid full rule explanations.

Examples of what paragraphs could touch on:
- The Core Puzzle
- The Solo System
- Where the Pressure Comes From
- Flow & Brain Burn

1-2 pictures. Close-up of main board. Player board. Moment that illustrates the core puzzle.

## The Solo Friction
Bullet points with short explanations. 150–200 words.

This is PERFECT for bullets.

Example:
- Automa upkeep: Simple, but requires frequent reference checks.
- Icon clarity: Took two plays before it felt intuitive.
- Table footprint: Larger than it looks on the box.
- Each bullet = 1–3 sentences max.

This keeps it sharp and prevents rant spirals.

## Why I’d Keep Playing (Staying power?)
Short paragraph, 150–200 words.
Replayability needs cohesion.

You’re answering: “Do I actually want to come back to this?”

That works better as a reflective paragraph than scattered bullets.

Keep it to ~150 words.

1 picture.

## Closing Thoughts
Play this when you’re in the mood for:
- Tight, escalating pressure
- Careful multi-turn planning
- A system that doesn’t forgive sloppy sequencing

Maybe skip it if you’re craving:
- A relaxed engine-builder
- Tactical flexibility
- A forgiving learning curve

1-2 decisive paragraph, 100–150 words.
Confident. Clear. Short.

This should feel like a conclusion, not a summary.

If you find yourself re-explaining things here, you’ve drifted.

This answers: After sitting with it, where does it fit in my life?

It’s about:
- Longevity
- Table time
- Comparison to others
- Mood suitability
- Collection impact

It’s about placement.
